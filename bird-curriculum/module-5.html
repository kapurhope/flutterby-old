Module 5 of 6

When Data Lies

Yesterday’s AI was pretty good. Today we feed it bad data on purpose.

Hands-on + discussion 45–55 min Bias & limits

Objectives

  • See how biased samples create misleading rules.
  • Connect that idea to citizen science and real AI limits.
  • Prefer AI + human over AI alone.

Materials

  • Biased nest dataset
  • Optional photo/cards: goldfinch-style yellow bird, penguin, bat vs bird

Instructions

“Today we feed the AI bad data on purpose.”

Part A — Biased nest data (20 min)

Almost every “Nest = Yes” comes from Riverside Park. Farm and city sites were barely checked.

Students rebuild a rule — it may overfit nonsense (“nests need a playground”).

Ask: “Can your AI still make good predictions for the whole region?”

Land this line: AI doesn’t know what’s true. It finds patterns in what we gave it.

Part B — Look-alike trick (15 min)

  • Bright yellow bird that is healthy vs a bad rule that says yellow = sick.
  • Or penguins breaking “birds fly.”
  • Optional: bat vs bird classification chaos.

Ask: “Did the AI fail, or did we teach it the wrong thing?”

Part C — Tool, not boss (8 min)

“Would you rather one person check 10,000 trees for nests, or cameras flag 200 spots for a human to check?”

Emphasize: AI helps humans look closer — it doesn’t replace care or fieldwork.

Wrap (5 min)

Exit ticket: “One way data can lie is ___.”

Assessment

Short paragraph: describe the biased set + one fix (sample more places, drop useless features, keep a human in the loop).
