# Module 1 — Build the Best Bird Beak
**Time:** 45–55 min · **Mode:** Hands-on · **Grades:** 5–8 · **Supplies:** beak tools, “foods,” trays, timers, score sheets

## Hook (3 min)
“Birds don’t have hands. Their beaks are tools. Today you *are* the bird.”

## Goals
- Collect food with an assigned “beak” under changing conditions.  
- Record data the class will reuse in Module 2.  
- Connect results to **variation → environment → who does well**.

## Setup
**Beaks (one per team or rotate):** spoon, tweezers, fork  

*Why only three?* Clear jobs for sixth graders — scoop (spoon), pick (tweezers), spear/grab (fork) — so Module 2’s “teach the computer” rules stay easy to see. 

**Foods (separate piles):**  
- Seeds → beans / rice  
- Worms → rubber bands  
- Soft fruit → pom-poms  
- Floating food → paper clips or pom-poms in a shallow water tray  

## Procedure

### Round A — Normal year (8 min)
1. Assign each team one beak (fairness > choice).  
2. 30 seconds: collect as much as you can of **one food type**, then rotate foods or run separate timed trials.  
3. Simpler version: one mixed pile; count pieces by type after 30s.  
4. Record on `handouts/beak-score-sheet.pdf`.

### Round B — Drought (6 min)
“Only large seeds remain.” Remove worms/fruit/floating food; leave large beans only. Repeat 30s. Record.

### Round C — Flood (6 min)
“Most food is floating.” Use the water tray only. Repeat. Record.

### Debrief (12–15 min)
Prompts:
- Which beak “won” in each environment?  
- Did any beak win *all* rounds?  
- If the climate kept flooding, which birds might struggle?  

Introduce vocabulary lightly: **adaptation**, **variation**, **environment**, **trade-off**.

### Wrap (5 min)
Preview Module 2: “Tomorrow we teach a computer using *your* numbers.”

## 5th–6th vs 7th–8th
- Younger: focus on “best tool for the job.”  
- Older: discuss that no beak is universally best; link to niche.

## Assessment
Completed score sheet with three rounds; one sentence: “Our beak was best when ___ because ___.”
