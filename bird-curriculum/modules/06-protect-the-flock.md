# Module 6 — Protect the Flock (capstone)
**Time:** 50–70 min · **Mode:** Computer-aided (required) · **Sim:** [`flockquest/`](../../flockquest/)

## Why a website here
Paper portfolios get static fast. FlockQuest lets pairs flip actions, watch nest success / neighbor conflict / budget update live, stress-test with events, and save two runs for debate — same “trade-offs with evidence” muscle as PowerQuest.

## Prep
- Open `flockquest/index.html` locally, or after Pages: `https://kapurhope.github.io/flutterby/flockquest/`
- Projector for intro; student devices in pairs (or stations)
- Optional print: `handouts/capstone-scorecard.pdf` for offline notes during debate

## Goals on the board (match the site)
1. Nest success ≥ **75**  
2. Neighbor conflict ≤ **Medium**  
3. Budget ≤ **$100**

## Procedure

### Intro (5 min)
Demo one preset + one event on the projector. Show Pass / Not yet badges.

### Explore (20 min) — pairs
1. Run **Strategy A** (try “Habitat first”).  
2. Save the run.  
3. Run **Strategy B** (try “People first” or invent your own).  
4. Test at least one event: heat wave, late freeze, or bad survey data.  
5. Save the second run.

### Document (8 min)
Copy key numbers onto the scorecard (or screenshot saved runs).

### Mini-debate (12–15 min)
60–90 sec each: best **balance** of three goals. Class votes.

### Reflect (5 min)
Cite 2–3 numbers + how an event changed the plan.

## No-device fallback
Use the printed action menu on `capstone-scorecard.md` and calculate by hand — same goals, slower feedback.

## 5th–6th vs 7th–8th
- Younger: stick to presets; teacher leads one event.  
- Older: require testing “bad survey data” and explaining why surveys matter.

## Assessment
Two saved FlockQuest runs + reflection; look-fors: uses data · names trade-off · clear speaking.
