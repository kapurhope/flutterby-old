Flutterby Field School · Course pitch for Peninsula Bridge

Why teach AI thinking to 6th graders?

A joyful, high-energy curriculum where students learn how “smart” systems really work — using birds and ecology as the playground.

Artificial intelligence already shapes homework help, social media, hiring tools, and the Bay Area economy. Yet students from first-generation and low-income backgrounds are far less likely to get early, hands-on exposure to what AI is — and what it gets wrong. Without that foundation, they risk becoming permanent users of powerful systems instead of informed questioners, creators, and leaders.

Our goal: Increase diversity and inclusion in the AI-powered future by teaching AI literacy early — through play, movement, and clear thinking — so every Peninsula Bridge scholar can understand patterns, challenge biased results, and speak with evidence.

Why this course

Flutterby Field School leads with AI concepts sixth graders can actually grasp: a computer only follows the rules we give it, bad data makes bad predictions, and humans must stay in the loop. Birds and ecology make those ideas concrete and fun — beak races, food-web tug-of-war, and simple “teach the robot” games — without coding, expensive labs, or heavy jargon.

What students practice

  • Spot patterns and write simple rules
  • Test a rule, then break it on purpose
  • Explain when data (or AI) misleads
  • Argue from evidence, not guesses

Why birds?

  • High-energy labs kids remember
  • Real science in their own backyard
  • A friendly on-ramp to STEM identity
  • Data they create themselves → “AI”

The Peninsula Bridge connection

Peninsula Bridge empowers highly motivated, first-gen, low-income students toward college and career success. This course fits that continuum: it builds academic confidence, STEM belonging, and future-ready AI awareness in middle school — before pathways narrow. Scholars leave not only knowing more about living systems, but believing “I can understand the technology that will shape my future.”

Designed for energetic 6th graders

Short, active lessons teachers can run with simple materials. Priority experiences: bird-beak challenges and food-web games, paired with one clear AI hook each day. Optional capstone: a simple conservation trade-off sim (FlockQuest) for debate with numbers.
