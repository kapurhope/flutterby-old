Module 2 of 6

Teach the Computer Which Beak Wins

You already made a dataset. Now you are the AI — writing rules a computer could follow.

Computer optional 45–55 min Uses Module 1 data

Objectives

  • Find patterns that predict a good habitat from beak performance.
  • Predict habitats for mystery birds.
  • Notice useless features (noise) and wrong predictions.

Materials

  • Class data from Module 1 or the sample table below
  • Rule card and mystery bird cards
  • Optional: projector or shared spreadsheet to show the table

The “AI” is still the students’ rules — devices only help display data.

Instructions

“You already made a dataset. Now you are the AI.”

Step 1 — Pattern hunt (12 min)

Ask: “If you were a computer, what rule predicts habitat?” Example: If Water score ≥ 18 → Wetland; else Forest.

Step 2 — Mystery birds (10 min)

Groups predict Wetland or Forest; class votes; then reveal answers.

Step 3 — Choose features (10 min)

Board list: beak length, width, beak color, wing length, seed/worm scores, temperature…

Ask which you’d give an AI — and which is probably useless. Push: color is often noise.

Step 4 — Bad AI (10 min)

Show a model that picks Wetland because a bird is “pretty/blue,” or a bird that breaks their rule because of a hidden factor (nesting sites).

Ask: “Did the AI fail, or did we train it with the wrong idea?”

Wrap (5 min)

Exit ticket: “One thing AI needs from us is ___.”

Differentiation & assessment

5th–6th: one rule + oral trick discussion. · 7th–8th: feature / training / prediction; score how many mystery birds they got right.

Assess: rule card + 3 predictions + one sentence on a miss.
